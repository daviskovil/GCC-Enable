Most GCC leaders know the moment. A global sponsor asks, "Could your team own this?" What happens next depends less on the pitch and more on what HQ has already seen.

In our experience, global sponsors look for a handful of readiness signals before they move real ownership to India.

## 1. Managers who decide, not escalate

If every non-routine question still travels to HQ, sponsors read that as a centre that executes rather than owns. The fastest way to change that perception is to strengthen the middle layer of managers so they make, and explain, their own decisions.

## 2. A clean track record on the last transition

Sponsors remember how the previous transfer went. A transition with clear phases, honest risk reporting and agreed exit criteria makes the next conversation much easier.

## 3. Business understanding, not only technical depth

Teams that talk about outcomes, customers and cost, rather than tickets and effort, are seen as partners. This is often the biggest shift for people who have joined from services firms.

## 4. A credible view on AI

HQ increasingly expects the centre to have a point of view on how AI changes its own work. A leadership team with shared AI literacy and a simple plan stands out.

## 5. Relationships that work across time zones

Stakeholders want to feel heard and to see constructive pushback. Teams that run tight global meetings and communicate clearly build trust quickly.

## Where to start

Pick the one signal that is weakest today and work on it deliberately for a quarter. Then ask your sponsor what they have noticed.
